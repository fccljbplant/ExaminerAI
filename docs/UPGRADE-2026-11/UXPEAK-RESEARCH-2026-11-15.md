# UXPeak research refresh — 15 November 2026

**Scope:** Public UXPeak pages only (home, about, playbook landing, public search snippets). No paid playbook pages were opened or copied; no proprietary artwork, code or distinctive branded layouts are reproduced. Concepts are applied as unowned design fundamentals, consistent with the 11 November UXPEAK-RESEARCH-AND-DESIGN.md entry and DECISIONS-REGISTER §1 ("no copied Star Admin or UXPeak assets/code").

## What the research established

UXPeak's public positioning (uxpeak.com, retrieved 2026-11-15) repeats five outcome promises — polished/professional UI, lovable UX, retention, conversion, and "AI edge". The craft list behind them: **"spacing, hierarchy, contrast, copy, and interaction details make a screen feel polished and easy to trust."** Their marketing frames the same diagnosis repeatedly: *"Your UI isn't ugly — it's just unclear."* Hierarchy is described as four levers: **size, contrast, spacing and placement** (public search snippets of their educational posts).

The Playbook's public table of contents lists the fundamentals the book teaches: **visual hierarchy, proximity, clarity, alignment, contrast, simplicity, whitespace, layout, balance and harmony.**

## Principles adopted for TraineesAI (with source)

1. **Don't tell the status. Show it.** (UXPeak Dribbble principle, already cited in the 11 November entry.) Status belongs in visual state — chips, icons, progress, tone — not prose paragraphs. Applied: project status chips pair a label with the real counts underneath (KPI cards), never a sentence like "this project is in progress".

2. **Value-first, not label-first.** (Playbook landing before/after listing redesign.) Their "bad" card lists `Type: For sale · Price: £845,000 · Location: …`; their "great" card leads with the decisive value (`For sale · £845,000`), demotes labels, adds icons to compact facts, and ends with one clear CTA. Applied rule: the most decision-relevant value comes first, largest; labels shrink to metadata; one primary action per card.

3. **Lead with the one number that matters, plus a plain-language reading.** (Playbook landing recovery-screen before/after.) Their redesign: the score (82) huge, the qualitative word ("Optimal") beside it, composition shown as percentage bars. Applied rule: every KPI block = one hero number + one qualitative descriptor + context beneath — not a table of equal-weighted metrics.

4. **Hierarchy = size, contrast, spacing, placement.** (Public educational posts.) If a screen feels unclear, fix which element is biggest, which has the strongest tone, what has room to breathe, and what sits at the top. Applied rule: audit each surface for "one clearly primary element" before touching anything else.

5. **Empty states are retention surfaces.** (Home page: "Design onboarding, habit loops, empty states, and reactivation flows.") Empty states must tell the user what they'd gain and give the next action — not just say "Nothing here yet". Applied rule: every empty state pairs a plain-language value statement with a visible CTA.

6. **Users should understand faster and act with confidence.** (Conversion outcome wording.) Forms and CTAs: one primary button, visible affordance, immediate feedback. This reinforces the existing 44px-target and focus-outline work.
